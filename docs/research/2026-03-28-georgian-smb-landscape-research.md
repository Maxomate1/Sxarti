## 1. Georgian SMB Pain Points

### 1.1 Extremely Low E-Commerce Participation

- **Only 4% of Georgian companies** were engaged in e-commerce in 2024, vs. 21% in the EU.
- Only **3% of Georgian SMEs sell online**, compared to 20% in the EU.
- E-commerce penetration was just ~1.1% of total retail sales (vs. ~12% EU average).
- Despite this, the market grew **tenfold** from GEL 0.3bn (2018) to GEL 3.5bn (2024), reaching GEL 7.62bn in 9M2025 with **35% YoY growth**.

**Implication for Sxarti:** The vast majority of Georgian SMBs are NOT online yet. The addressable market is enormous, but the tool must be simple enough to onboard businesses with zero digital sales experience.

### 1.2 Poor Customer Experience on Local Platforms

- Most Georgian online retailers **lack basic features**: no price lists, no product availability indicators, no geographic coverage info.
- Georgians spend **3.3x more on international shops** (Temu, Amazon, AliExpress) than on local ones.
- In H1 2025, **Temu alone accounted for ~50% of e-commerce transaction volumes** in Georgia.

**Implication for Sxarti:** Local businesses are losing to international platforms because their digital presence is primitive. Sxarti can help them compete by providing a sophisticated AI sales layer on top of their existing social media presence.

### 1.3 Logistics and Delivery Gaps

- Delivery speed, regional coverage outside Tbilisi, and courier shortages are the top operational pain points (per Galt & Taggart).
- International orders require freight-forwarding (2-3 weeks), customs, and 18% VAT on orders above GEL 300.

### 1.4 Digital Skills and Infrastructure Gaps

- Only **50% of Georgian SMEs** have broadband speeds of at least 30 Mbps (vs. 89% EU).
- Just **7% of SMEs** have websites with advanced features (vs. 78% EU).
- Georgian SMEs lag EU peers in: **ERP (7% vs 42%)**, **social media (30% vs 56%)**, and **AI (2% vs 13%)**.
- Significant digital divide between Tbilisi and rural regions.
- Shortage of digitally skilled workers across the economy.

**Implication for Sxarti:** The tool must work where businesses already are (Facebook/Instagram), not require them to build websites or learn complex systems. Mobile-first, zero-code setup is essential.

### 1.5 Access to Finance

- Financing remains a major obstacle for emerging businesses.
- Government grants available (up to GEL 30,000 via GITA micro/small enterprise programs), but reach is limited.
- ProCredit Bank and others offer SME educational sessions, but practical digital tool adoption remains low.

## 2. Competitor Analysis

### 2.1 International Chatbot Platforms

#### ManyChat

- **Strengths:** Multi-channel (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Telegram, WhatsApp, SMS), clean UI, strong marketing automation, Instagram DM automation.
- **Pricing:** Free tier available; Pro from $15/month.
- **Georgian relevance:** No Georgian language UI. No local payment integrations. No Georgian-language chatbot templates. Requires English literacy to set up and manage.
- **Key gap for Georgia:** Does not understand Georgian consumer patterns, no integration with BOG/TBC payment systems.

#### Tidio

- **Strengths:** Free CRM with live chat + chatbot, works on websites, AI-powered Lyro chatbot for customer service.
- **Pricing:** Free tier; paid from $29/month.
- **Georgian relevance:** Website-focused (most Georgian SMBs don't have websites). No Georgian language UI or templates.
- **Key gap for Georgia:** Assumes businesses have a website, which 93% of Georgian SMEs lack with advanced features.

#### Chatfuel

- **Strengths:** Simplest setup among major platforms, Facebook/Instagram focused, no-code builder.
- **Pricing:** From $14.39/month.
- **Georgian relevance:** Closest to Sxarti's use case (Facebook/Instagram focus), but no Georgian language support, no local integrations.

#### MeaChat

- **Strengths:** E-commerce platform specifically for Facebook & Instagram selling, product catalog management.
- **Pricing:** Varies.
- **Georgian relevance:** Similar concept to Sxarti but no Georgian language or local market adaptation.

### 2.2 Local Georgian Digital Service Providers

#### myGO (mygo.ge)

- Digital Innovation Agency based in Tbilisi (12+ years).
- Offers AI Integration, Chatbot Systems, Machine Learning, software development.
- Provides **custom solutions**, not a SaaS product. Likely expensive (agency rates $50-120/hr).
- Not a direct competitor to Sxarti's self-service SaaS model.

#### Other Tbilisi Agencies

- Multiplayer, WATSON, Punch Creative, PPC Magic — all digital marketing agencies.
- Focus on ads, content, SMM management. None offer self-service AI chatbot SaaS.
- Typical engagement model: agency retainer, not monthly SaaS subscription.

## 3. Social Commerce Trends in Georgia

### 3.1 Social Media Penetration

- **2.85 million social media users** in Georgia (January 2024) = **76.6% of total population**.
- Facebook and YouTube are the dominant platforms.
- Instagram used by **53.8% of Shopify stores** in Tbilisi — the most popular social platform for online retailers.
- TV viewership declining, internet/social media usage increasing, especially among younger demographics.
- 55+ age group has lowest social media usage; Facebook and YouTube are most common for this group.

### 3.2 How Georgians Shop via Social Media

- Many small businesses operate **exclusively through Facebook pages and Instagram profiles**.
- Common pattern: Business posts products on Facebook/Instagram -> Customer comments or sends DM -> Business owner manually responds -> Order confirmed via chat -> Payment arranged separately -> Delivery coordinated.
- This process is **entirely manual**, creating a massive bottleneck as businesses scale.
- **28% of Tbilisi Shopify stores sell Apparel**, 11.6% Home & Garden, 9.3% Beauty & Fitness — these are the dominant social commerce categories.

### 3.3 Local E-Commerce Platforms

- **Mymarket.ge**: Largest platform (C2C focus), 1M+ monthly unique visitors. Primarily used marketplace, similar to eBay.
- **Extra.ge**: Largest B2C marketplace, ~500K monthly visitors.
- **Vendoo.ge**: ~230K monthly visitors.
- **Be.ge**: ~130K monthly visitors.
- Many businesses use these platforms AND social media simultaneously, with no integration between channels.

### 3.4 Payment Landscape

#### Digital Payment Adoption (Strong and Growing)

- **90.71% of card transactions** in H1 2025 are payments (up from 83.66% in H1 2022).
- Digital banking channels now account for **over 95% of customer transactions**.
- Mobile wallets gaining traction, especially among younger demographics.

#### Key Payment Players

- **TBC Bank / TBC Pay**: Major digital payments player. TBC Pay bridges banking infrastructure with widespread consumer adoption across all socioeconomic tiers. 75% of TBC clients make e-commerce payments on Georgian websites.
- **Bank of Georgia (BOG)**: Leading bank with strong digital infrastructure, BOG Pay widely used.
- **Card payments**: Visa and Mastercard widely accepted.
- **Cash on delivery**: Still used, particularly outside major cities, but declining rapidly.

#### Payment Preferences

- Most popular: local bank debit/credit cards, mobile wallets, direct bank transfers.
- Large sums: many still prefer cash.
- Rural areas: cash still common, but frequency declining.

**Implication for Sxarti:** Integrating with BOG and TBC payment systems is a significant competitive advantage. Digital payment infrastructure is mature enough to support automated checkout flows.

## 4. AI Chatbot Adoption Barriers

### 4.1 Georgian Language as a Technical Barrier

- Georgian (ქართული) is classified as a **low-resource language** for NLP.
- Spoken by only ~4 million people worldwide.
- Written in the unique **Mkhedruli script** — not Latin, Cyrillic, or Arabic-based.
- Key NLP challenges: **agglutinative morphology, split-ergative case system, polypersonal verb agreement, free word order**.
- Limited parallel corpora for machine translation training.
- Resources have expanded significantly since 2022, but still far behind major languages.

**Current AI support for Georgian:**

- **Google Gemini**: Supports Georgian (ka) across all models. Gemma 3/3n supports 140+ languages including Georgian.
- **OpenAI/ChatGPT**: Georgian language support has been a community request, with mixed results.
- **ElevenLabs**: Georgian text-to-speech available.
- **Gladia**: Georgian speech-to-text available.

**Implication for Sxarti:** Using Gemini as the AI backbone is a strategic advantage — it has the best Georgian language support among major LLMs. However, the chatbot must be tested extensively for Georgian-specific edge cases (colloquialisms, dialects, code-switching with Russian/English).

### 4.2 Trust and Cultural Barriers

- **Low consumer trust** in online transactions remains a constraining factor.
- Preference for personal, human interaction in sales (culturally important in Georgia).
- Skepticism toward AI — "will the chatbot understand what my customer wants?"
- Business owners may fear losing the personal touch that builds customer loyalty.
- Only **2% of Georgian SMEs use AI** (vs. 13% EU average).

### 4.3 Cost Sensitivity

- Georgia's GDP per capita is approximately $7,600 (2024).
- Average monthly salary in Tbilisi: ~GEL 1,800-2,500 ($650-900).
- Small business owners are extremely price-sensitive.
- SaaS subscriptions in USD/EUR are perceived as expensive.
- **Sxarti pricing in GEL** (Starter 49, Business 149, Premium 299) is well-positioned for the market, but even 49 GEL/month needs clear ROI justification.

### 4.4 Technical Complexity and Digital Literacy

- Many small business owners have limited technical skills.
- Setting up international tools (ManyChat, Chatfuel) requires English proficiency.
- Integration with Facebook Business API is technically complex.
- Business owners are used to doing everything manually via phone and direct messages.
- Fear of "breaking something" or losing control over customer relationships.

### 4.5 Regulatory and Infrastructure Concerns

- Underdeveloped data protection frameworks (though improving with EU association).
- Questions about AI data handling and customer privacy.
- Internet reliability issues outside Tbilisi.

### 4.6 Emerging Market AI Adoption Research (Global Context)

Per OECD and academic research on SMEs in emerging markets:

- **55% of firms** report talent gap as the strongest barrier to AI adoption.
- Tools are often **too complicated** or not aligned with SME workflows.
- **Crowded market** of solutions, but few tailored for SME realities.
- Insufficient practical training — often too theoretical or inaccessible.
- Most research focuses on Europe/North America, leaving knowledge gaps for countries like Georgia.

## 5. Strategic Implications for Sxarti

### 5.1 Market Opportunity Summary

| Metric                                        | Value                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Georgian social media users                   | 2.85M (76.6% of population) |
| E-commerce market (9M2025)                    | GEL 7.62B (+35% YoY)        |
| SMEs engaged in e-commerce                    | Only 4%                     |
| SMEs using AI                                 | Only 2%                     |
| Direct competitors (Georgian AI chatbot SaaS) | **Zero**                    |
| Market forecast (2030)                        | GEL 10.7B                   |

### 5.2 Key Strategic Advantages

1. **First-mover advantage**: No competitor offers AI sales chatbot automation in Georgian. This is a greenfield market.
2. **Georgian language**: The single biggest moat. International tools cannot serve this market effectively.
3. **GEL pricing**: Local currency pricing removes friction and perceived expense.
4. **Social-first approach**: Meeting businesses where they already are (Facebook/Instagram), not requiring them to build websites.
5. **Payment integration**: BOG/TBC integration is a differentiator no international tool offers.

### 5.3 Key Risks and Challenges

1. **Market education**: 98% of Georgian SMEs have never used AI. Sxarti must educate, not just sell.
2. **Trust building**: Demos, free trials, case studies in Georgian are essential.
3. **Georgian NLP quality**: Must be flawless. One bad conversation in Georgian could destroy trust.
4. **Price sensitivity**: Even at 49 GEL, clear ROI demonstration is needed (e.g., "saves you 4 hours/day of manual DM responses").
5. **Digital literacy**: Onboarding must be extremely simple — video tutorials in Georgian, WhatsApp-based support.

### 5.4 Recommended Focus Areas

1. **Target verticals**: Apparel (28% of social commerce), Beauty & Fitness (9.3%), Home & Garden (11.6%), Food delivery.
2. **Geographic focus**: Start with Tbilisi (highest digital adoption), expand to Batumi, Kutaisi.
3. **Key messaging**: "Your AI sales assistant that speaks Georgian" / "Never miss a customer message again."
4. **Onboarding**: Georgian-language video walkthrough, 1-click Facebook page connection, pre-built templates for top verticals.
5. **Pricing strategy**: Consider a free tier or extended trial to overcome trust barriers. The 49 GEL Starter plan should have a 14-30 day free trial.
